Privacy Policy

Template for self-hosters. This page is a starting point, not a legal document, and it is not legal advice. The sections describing what the software does are accurate as written and identical on every instance. The fields marked OPERATOR are the ones only you can fill in, because they depend on your instance: your identity, your jurisdiction, your subprocessors, your age threshold, and any retention period longer than the built-in ones. If you run an instance, you are the controller of the data on it and you should publish your own policy.

Template version 1.0 · Edit before publishing

1. What the software processes

An instance stores what you give it and what the service needs to run:

  • Account data — username, display name, optional email address, and a bcrypt hash of your password. The password itself is never stored. The version of the Terms and Privacy Policy you accepted, and when, is recorded against your account.
  • Profile data — status text, bio, avatar and banner images, community icon and banner images, and the files you attach to messages.
  • Content — the messages you send, in communities and in direct messages, plus their edit and deletion history, and your reactions, pins and mutes.
  • Social graph — which communities you joined, which roles you hold there, and your friendships and friend requests.
  • Moderation records — reports you file, appeals you submit, moderation actions taken against accounts or communities, and an audit log of administrative actions.
  • Short-lived tokens — password reset and email verification tokens, stored only as a SHA-256 hash, and a denylist of revoked session tokens.

The application server does not keep a request log of its own. If this instance sits behind a reverse proxy such as nginx or Caddy, that proxy may record IP addresses and request paths; what it keeps, and for how long, is the operator's configuration, not the software's.

2. Why it is processed

  • To provide the service — running the software requires storing your account and the content you send. This rests on your acceptance of the Terms.
  • To keep the service secure — revoking sessions, rate limiting, enforcing role permissions, and investigating abuse.
  • To meet your requests — delivering password reset and email verification mail, and answering a data subject request.
  • Consent — where you supply an email address, so that we can contact you about that account.

The specific lawful basis under the law that applies to your instance is OPERATOR: state the lawful basis under your jurisdiction — for example performance of a contract, legitimate interest, or consent.

3. Who else sees it

Personal information is not sold, and it is not used to train models. It is visible to other people only where the product requires it: other members of a community can read the messages you post there and see your display name, avatar and roles; the recipient of a direct message sees what you send them. A community's moderators can act on its members and see its message history.

Outside that, an operator may rely on processors — for example an email provider, if SMTP is configured for password resets, and a hosting provider. OPERATOR: list your processors here, and what each may access. A self-hosted instance with no SMTP configured sends no email at all.

4. Storage, including off the host

By default, files are written to a directory on the machine running the server. An operator may instead configure an S3-compatible object store — Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2, MinIO, or another provider. Either way the bucket or directory is the operator's to secure, and the software expects it to be private: every file is served through an authenticated route that checks the viewer's access first, so a publicly readable bucket would expose attachments that the software would otherwise gate.

5. Retention

These periods are built into the software and apply on every instance:

  • Revoked session tokens — purged hourly, once past the session's own expiry.
  • Password reset and email verification tokens — purged hourly, once used or expired.
  • Uploaded files not yet attached to a message — deleted after 24 hours, along with their database record.

Everything else — messages, communities, roles, moderation and audit records — is kept until it is deleted or until the account is erased. The software implements no other automatic expiry. OPERATOR: state any retention period longer than these, and your backup retention. Trycord does not manage backups, and a backup taken before an erasure will still contain the data until that backup is discarded.

6. Erasure

You can ask for your account to be deleted from Settings → Account → Delete my account. The request requires your password and is reviewed by an administrator before it runs. When it runs:

  • Erased: your username, email address, display name, bio, avatar and banner, every file you uploaded, your sessions, friendships, friend requests and notifications. Your username is replaced with a placeholder, so it stays unique and cannot be reassigned to someone else.
  • Kept: the messages you posted, and any moderation or audit record that names you. These are kept so that other people's conversations remain intact and so that action taken against your account, or by your account, stays on the record. They resolve to a deleted account rather than to you.
  • Transferred: a community you owned is handed to its longest-standing remaining member, so the people still using it are not left without an owner.

You can withdraw a request before it is processed, from the same screen. Every request and decision is written to the audit log.

7. Security measures in the software

  • Passwords are hashed with bcrypt at a work factor of 12, configurable per instance.
  • Sessions are signed tokens with a per-token identifier, so an individual session can be revoked and all sessions can be revoked at once. Revoking sessions also disconnects any live connection opened by the old session.
  • Permissions are enforced on the server for every request, not in the client. Role hierarchy limits who may grant, edit, reorder or remove which role.
  • Uploads are identified by their contents, not by the file name the sender supplied. Images, PDFs and a few text formats are served inline; a file the server cannot place is stored and served as a download, so a browser is never asked to interpret bytes the server did not recognise. Avatars, banners and community icons must be images.
  • A Content-Security-Policy is sent with client resources, inline scripts are not permitted, and the origin allowed to call the API is configurable.
  • Administrative actions, moderation actions and erasure decisions are written to an audit log.

Transport encryption is the operator's responsibility: Trycord speaks plain HTTP and expects to sit behind a proxy that terminates TLS, except in local development. No method of transmission is perfect, and the operator cannot guarantee absolute security.

8. Your rights

Depending on your jurisdiction you may be entitled to access, correct, delete, export or restrict processing of your personal information, and to object to it. In the app you can read and change your own profile, revoke sessions, and request deletion as described above. For anything else, contact OPERATOR: your privacy contact address.

9. Children

The software does not enforce an age threshold; it has no age check and no mechanism for verifying a user's age. Operators who serve children are responsible for the threshold they apply and for enforcing it. OPERATOR: state your age threshold, or state that the service is not directed at children.

10. International transfers

OPERATOR: state where this instance is hosted, and where any processor named in section 3 is located, if data is transferred outside your jurisdiction.

11. Changes

The software versions this policy, and records on your account the version you accepted and when. When a new version is published, users are asked to accept it before it takes effect.

12. Contact

Privacy questions about this instance go to OPERATOR: your privacy contact address.
